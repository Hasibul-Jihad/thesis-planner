Fine-tune a pretrained HuBERT or wav2vec 2.0 model to classify emotions (happy, sad, angry, neutral, fear, disgust, surprise) from Bangla speech. Bangla is your native language — this is a real research gap. You don't train from scratch; pretrained models do the heavy lifting.

Month 1 — Python & ML Foundations · 44 hours
Learn to code. Understand what ML is. Do not touch audio yet.
Weeks 1–2 (22 hrs): Python fundamentals — variables, loops, functions, lists, dicts. Use Claude/ChatGPT to explain every error. Work through freeCodeCamp Python or CS50P daily. Goal: write a script that reads a CSV and prints averages.
Week 3 (11 hrs): NumPy and Pandas basics. Load data, slice arrays, compute statistics. Kaggle micro-courses are free and take ~1 hr each.
Week 4 (11 hrs): Watch 3Blue1Brown Neural Networks playlist (8 videos). Read Chapters 1–2 of the ML guide. No coding this week — understanding only.
Month 1 Checkpoint
You can write a Python script that loads data, filters it, and prints results without help.
Month 2 — Audio Signal Processing · 44 hours
Learn the language of audio. Build your first audio classifier.
Week 1 (11 hrs): Valerio Velardo — Audio Signal Processing for ML playlist, first 8 videos. Re-watch with a Colab notebook open. Plot waveforms and spectrograms in Python.
Week 2 (11 hrs): MFCCs and Mel spectrograms using librosa. Extract features from 10 audio files. Understand what each feature means physically.
Week 3 (11 hrs): HuggingFace Audio Course, Section 1 — Audio classification. Copy the tutorial exactly; understanding comes from running it.
Week 4 (11 hrs): Download ESC-50. Build a mel spectrogram + CNN classifier end to end. Doesn't need good accuracy — just working.
Month 2 Checkpoint
You can load any audio file, extract a mel spectrogram, and run it through a classifier.
Month 3 — Literature Review & Topic Lock · 44 hours
Read 5 papers. Lock your scope. Write introduction and literature review.
Week 1 (11 hrs): Read 5 papers: wav2vec 2.0 (arxiv 2006.11477), HuBERT (arxiv 2106.07447), SUBESCO dataset paper, + 2 papers on Bangla speech emotion recognition. Ask AI to explain any paragraph you don't understand.
Week 2 (11 hrs): Finalise research question with supervisor. Write thesis introduction (1–2 pages). Question: "How does fine-tuned HuBERT compare to a CNN baseline for Bangla SER?"
Week 3 (11 hrs): Write literature review (4–6 pages). Cover: speech emotion recognition, transfer learning for audio, Bangla speech processing state of the art.
Week 4 (11 hrs): Download SUBESCO dataset. Explore its structure. Write methodology chapter outline.
Month 3 Checkpoint

Research question finalised. Introduction + literature review drafted. Dataset downloaded and explored.

Month 4 — Baseline Implementation · 44 hours
Build CNN baseline. Fine-tune HuBERT. Run first experiments.
Week 1 (11 hrs): Preprocess SUBESCO: load audio, extract mel spectrograms, create 70/15/15 train/val/test splits. Write the data loading pipeline.
Week 2 (11 hrs): Build CNN baseline: mel spectrogram → 3-layer CNN → emotion class. Train on Colab free GPU. Record accuracy and F1 per emotion class.
Week 3 (11 hrs): Fine-tune HuBERT using HuggingFace Trainer API. Use the audio course fine-tuning tutorial as your exact template.
Week 4 (11 hrs): Compare CNN vs HuBERT: accuracy, F1, confusion matrix per emotion. Write methodology chapter. Produce results table.
Month 4 Checkpoint
Both models trained. Results table complete. Methodology chapter written.
Month 5 — Analysis, Ablations & Results Writing · 44 hours
Dig into results. Run extra experiments. Write results and discussion.
Week 1 (11 hrs): Error analysis: which emotions are confused? Listen to misclassified clips. Try data augmentation (pitch shift, noise) and retrain.
Week 2 (11 hrs): Ablation: what happens with 50% / 25% of training data? How does HuBERT degrade vs CNN? This is a genuine contribution at BSc level.
Weeks 3–4 (22 hrs): Write results chapter (all tables, confusion matrix figures) and discussion chapter (why does HuBERT outperform? what are the limitations?).
Month 5 Checkpoint
Results + discussion chapters complete. Thesis ~70% written.
Month 6 — Writing, Polishing & Submission · 44 hours
Write conclusion. Revise everything. Submit.
Week 1 (11 hrs): Write conclusion: what you found, why it matters, limitations, future work. Write abstract last — easiest when done.
Week 2 (11 hrs): Full readthrough — fix structure, transitions, grammar. Every figure needs a caption. Every claim needs a citation.
Week 3 (11 hrs): Supervisor review round. Apply feedback. Format references (IEEE or APA per your department).
Week 4 (11 hrs): Buffer week — final supervisor comments, viva/defence slides if required, final submission.

The Golden Rule
Phone off. One task only. No switching. 1 quality hour beats 3 distracted hours every time. Use Pomodoro: 25 min work → 5 min break → 25 min work = your 1 hour.
Weekday Session · 1 Hour (Mon–Fri)
0:00 – 0:05
Open yesterday's work. Read your last 3 lines of code or last paragraph. Remind yourself exactly where you were. Do NOT start anything new.
0:05 – 0:30
Deep work block 1. Code one function, read one paper section, or write one paragraph. One thing only.
0:30 – 0:35
Break. Stand up. Walk. No phone, no social media.
0:35 – 0:55
Deep work block 2. Continue or move to next small task.
0:55 – 1:00
Write 3 sentences in a notes file: what you did · what's blocking you · what you'll do tomorrow. This replaces having to remember next time.
Weekend Session · 3 Hours (Sat & Sun)
0:00 – 0:10
Review your weekly notes. Set one clear goal: "By end of today I will have X done." Write it down.
0:10 – 1:10
Deep work block 1 (60 min). Hardest task first — coding, writing, or building.
1:10 – 1:20
Break. Walk or eat. No screens.
1:20 – 2:20
Deep work block 2 (60 min). Continue or switch to second task.
2:20 – 2:30
Break.
2:30 – 2:50
Polish and organise: clean code, fix notes, update progress tracker.
2:50 – 3:00
Plan next week. Write 5 specific tasks for Mon–Sun. Specific beats vague: "fine-tune HuBERT for 10 epochs and record F1" not "work on model".
When you're stuck

1. Paste the error into Claude or ChatGPT — explain what you wanted. 90% of issues resolve this way.
2. If stuck for more than 2 sessions: simplify the goal. Don't fight one bug for a week — reduce scope and move forward.

The Most Important Thing
Consistency beats intensity. 11 hours a week every week for 26 weeks = 286 hours of genuine work. That is more than enough. One missed week is fine. Two consecutive missed weeks = you are behind. Protect Month 1 and 2 — they have the most slack.
On using AI to help you code
Using Claude or ChatGPT to write and explain code is fine and expected at BSc level — as long as you understand every line you submit. Ask AI to write something, then ask it to explain each line. If you can explain your code to your supervisor, you know it well enough. Never copy-paste without reading.
The 3 mistakes that kill BSc theses
Scope creepTrying to build too much. ONE research question. ONE clear answer. Not five experiments.
Skip lit reviewSupervisors fail theses that ignore prior work. Month 3 is not optional.
Bulk writingLeaving all writing to Month 6. Write one paragraph every day from Month 3 onwards.
How to talk to your supervisor
Meet at least once a month. Come with: (1) what you did, (2) what result you got, (3) one specific problem you're facing. Ask one specific question. Supervisors respect students who show work even when it's wrong. They cannot help students who disappear.
When to relax vs act fast
✓ Relax ifModel doesn't converge in Week 1 · Accuracy is low at first · You don't understand a paper fully · One bug takes 3 sessions to fix. All normal.
⚡ Act fast ifNo supervisor meeting by end of Month 3 · Dataset unavailable · Zero pages written by end of Month 3 · 3+ consecutive weeks missed.
Your entire thesis in one sentence

"I found that fine-tuned HuBERT outperforms CNN baselines for Bangla speech emotion recognition, with X% accuracy on the SUBESCO dataset, suggesting that self-supervised pretrained models transfer effectively to low-resource emotional speech tasks even with limited fine-tuning data."

Keep this sentence visible at all times. Everything you do over 6 months supports or refines it.

The Most Important Thing
Consistency beats intensity. 11 hours a week every week for 26 weeks = 286 hours of genuine work. That is more than enough. One missed week is fine. Two consecutive missed weeks = you are behind. Protect Month 1 and 2 — they have the most slack.
On using AI to help you code
Using Claude or ChatGPT to write and explain code is fine and expected at BSc level — as long as you understand every line you submit. Ask AI to write something, then ask it to explain each line. If you can explain your code to your supervisor, you know it well enough. Never copy-paste without reading.
The 3 mistakes that kill BSc theses
Scope creepTrying to build too much. ONE research question. ONE clear answer. Not five experiments.
Skip lit reviewSupervisors fail theses that ignore prior work. Month 3 is not optional.
Bulk writingLeaving all writing to Month 6. Write one paragraph every day from Month 3 onwards.
How to talk to your supervisor
Meet at least once a month. Come with: (1) what you did, (2) what result you got, (3) one specific problem you're facing. Ask one specific question. Supervisors respect students who show work even when it's wrong. They cannot help students who disappear.
When to relax vs act fast
✓ Relax ifModel doesn't converge in Week 1 · Accuracy is low at first · You don't understand a paper fully · One bug takes 3 sessions to fix. All normal.
⚡ Act fast ifNo supervisor meeting by end of Month 3 · Dataset unavailable · Zero pages written by end of Month 3 · 3+ consecutive weeks missed.

📚 Priority Reading List — What to Read, When, and How Deeply
The honest truth about reading papers as a beginner
You do not need to understand every equation. Read the Abstract → Introduction → Results → Conclusion first. Only go into the methodology if you are implementing that exact approach. A paper you "skim well" is more useful than a paper you avoided because it looked hard.
How to read a paper — the 3-pass method
Pass 1 · 10 minSkim: Read title, abstract, intro first paragraph, all headings, conclusion. Answer: What problem? What did they do? Did it work? This is enough for papers you only need to cite.
Pass 2 · 30 minRead: Read intro fully, figures and tables, results, conclusion. Skip the math. Answer: How did they do it? What dataset? What metric? What result? Do this for your 5 core papers.
Pass 3 · 2 hrsStudy: Read every section including methodology and equations. Take notes. Answer: Could you implement this? Do this only for the 1–2 papers whose exact method you are replicating.
